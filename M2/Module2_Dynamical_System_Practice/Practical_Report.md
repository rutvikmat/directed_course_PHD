## Estimation and Stability Analysis of a Dynamical System with Machine Learning Forecast Comparison

**Module:** Dynamical Systems and Data-Driven System Identification

## 1. Aim
To estimate a mathematical dynamical system from simulated observations, analyse its equilibrium points and local stability, and compare its forecasts with a conventional machine-learning model.

## 2. System and mathematical model
A continuous-time logistic growth system is used:
\[
\frac{dx}{dt} = rx\left(1-\frac{x}{K}\right)
\]
where \(x(t)\) is the system state, \(r\) is the growth-rate parameter, and \(K\) is the carrying capacity. The simulation uses true parameters \(r=0.20\), \(K=100\), and initial state \(x(0)=10\). Gaussian observation noise with standard deviation 1.5 is added to the simulated states.

## 3. Data and experimental procedure
A trajectory is simulated for 101 time steps using a numerical ODE solver. Noisy observations are saved to `outputs/simulated_data.csv`. The observations are split chronologically: the first 70% for training and the remaining 30% for testing.

The logistic parameters are estimated from the training observations by nonlinear least squares using the closed-form logistic solution. The identified model is then used to forecast from the final training state over the test horizon.

## 4. Conventional machine-learning model
A Random Forest regressor is trained to predict the next state from the five preceding observations. During testing, predictions are generated recursively: each prediction is appended to the history and used to produce the next forecast.

## 5. Stability analysis
The system function is \(f(x)=rx(1-x/K)\). Equilibrium points satisfy \(f(x^*)=0\), giving:
\[
x^*_1=0,\qquad x^*_2=K.
\]
The Jacobian is:
\[
J(x)=f'(x)=r\left(1-\frac{2x}{K}\right).
\]
For this one-dimensional system, the Jacobian eigenvalue at each equilibrium is its derivative evaluated at that point. A positive eigenvalue indicates local instability; a negative eigenvalue indicates local asymptotic stability.

Estimated-parameter stability results:

|   equilibrium |   jacobian_eigenvalue | local_stability       |
|--------------:|----------------------:|:----------------------|
|          0    |              0.191771 | Unstable              |
|         99.97 |             -0.191771 | Asymptotically stable |

Thus, the zero-state equilibrium is unstable and the carrying-capacity equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable, provided the estimated growth rate and carrying capacity are positive.

## 6. Forecast evaluation
Both models are evaluated against the same held-out observations using MAE, RMSE and R².

|                            |    MAE |   RMSE |      R2 |
|:---------------------------|-------:|-------:|--------:|
| Identified logistic system | 1.1433 | 1.488  | -0.1118 |
| Random Forest              | 1.1027 | 1.4631 | -0.0749 |

Lower MAE and RMSE indicate smaller forecast errors. A higher R² indicates that a greater proportion of the variation in the test observations is explained. These numerical results are generated by the included reproducible Python script.

## 7. Results and discussion
The experiment demonstrates data-driven system identification: a known dynamical process is simulated, its parameters are estimated from noisy observations, and the identified model is used for forecasting. Stability analysis identifies the equilibria and classifies their local behaviour using Jacobian eigenvalues.

The forecast metrics compare an explicit dynamical equation with a data-driven Random Forest baseline. The model with lower MAE and RMSE (and, where appropriate, higher R²) performs better on this particular simulated test set. The result is specific to this simulation, noise level, split and model configuration.

## 8. Conclusion
The practical estimates a logistic dynamical system from simulated observations, examines its equilibrium points and local stability, and compares its forecasts with those of a Random Forest regressor. It illustrates how mathematical structure provides interpretable parameters and stability information, while conventional machine learning provides a data-driven forecasting baseline. The experiment fulfils the Module 2 practical requirements.
