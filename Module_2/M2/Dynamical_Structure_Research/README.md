## Forecast alignment and evaluation

Each row timestamp `t` uses observed demand through `t` and predicts demand at `t+1`. The 24-hour seasonal-naive predictor is `y[t-23]`. Data are split chronologically (70% train, 15% validation, 15% test). State-space parameters are fitted only through the training cutoff. Filtered state at time `t` is allowed to incorporate `y[t]`, which is available for the next-hour forecast. No smoothed future-informed states are used.

The state-space model is a local-level + daily seasonal (period 24) structural model. Its one-step-ahead forecasts are conditioned on observed history, so evaluation is **rolling one-step-ahead** rather than a fixed-origin multi-step forecast. This is an online-updating setting.

## Important scientific limitations

1. **Experiment 3 is a conditional ablation**, not a strict end-to-end data-efficiency study: the state-space estimator is fitted on the full training window for all training fractions. A fully controlled data-efficiency study must refit the state-space model for each fraction and regenerate its features without look-ahead.
2. **Experiment 4 is descriptive stratification** by realized test-set change magnitude. It is not an ex-ante regime detector and not a controlled distribution-shift experiment.
3. The state-space features and XGBoost features may capture overlapping seasonality. Hybrid gains should be compared against an equal-complexity, non-state feature augmentation.
4. One household cannot establish population-level generalization. Extend to other household datasets for stronger conclusions.
5. SHAP and feature importance do not alone prove interpretability; examine stability and semantic correspondence of inferred states.
6. Full-dataset state-space filtering and repeated tree fitting can take significant time. Begin with `--max-hours 12000 --fast`.
7. Results are generated by execution; no experimental results are pre-filled or fabricated.
